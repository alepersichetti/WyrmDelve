# How the traps are made

*[Versione italiana](trap.md)*

This document explains how WyrmDelve chooses, places and describes the traps of a dungeon, with real examples generated by the program.

> Traps only appear if at the start, at "What do you want to make?", you also choose the **traps** (number `3`, or `--content traps`, also together with other things: `--content story,traps`). How the map they are placed on is made is explained in [dungeon_map_generation.en.md](dungeon_map_generation.en.md).

## 1. The idea: a trap is a problem, not a dice roll

The logic follows the principles of the post [*Some Traps*](https://goblinpunch.blogspot.com/2018/08/some-traps.html) by Goblin Punch (Arnold K.):

- **learn first**: before a hidden trap, the players must have seen the same mechanism exposed or broken;
- **telegraph the mechanism, not the danger**: there is always something to notice (seams, holes, a lever oiled too well), even if you don't know what will happen;
- **solve it by thinking**: with what they see, the players can avoid it, disarm it or turn it to their advantage, without relying on a dice roll.

That's why every trap, in the key, has four parts:

| Part | What it says | Example (hidden pit) |
|---|---|---|
| **name** | what it is | hidden pit |
| **tell** | what you notice, if you look | the floor slabs have straight seams and sound hollow |
| **effect** | what happens if it goes off | the floor opens: a deep fall onto a stone bottom |
| **countermeasures** | how to beat it by thinking | probe the floor with a pole; jump it; nail the lid shut |

All the traps are in `data/traps.json`. The post's principles are used, not its text. WyrmDelve's own traps, the triggers, the mechanisms and the twists are written for WyrmDelve; 34 ready-made traps (marked `"source": "srd35"`) are adapted from the sample traps of the *System Reference Document 3.5*, Open Game Content under the OGL 1.0a (see `LICENSE-OGL.txt`), the same ones [donjon](https://donjon.bin.sh)'s generator is based on: the same mechanisms (fusillade of darts, wall scythe, collapsing column, crushing room…), the text rewritten for old-school play, with no skill checks and no numbers but the dice.

## 2. Families and the three stages

A **family** is a mechanism. Each family has up to three **stages**:

| Stage | What it is |
|---|---|
| **0 — broken or exposed** | it does no harm: it shows how the mechanism works |
| **1 — working** | the hidden mechanism |
| **2 — cruel** | a variant that punishes whoever thinks they've figured it out |

The families, with the ready-made traps by stage and the mechanisms new ones are assembled from (chapter 8):

|---|---|---|---|---|

| `block` blocks | 1 / 3 / 4 | 2 (0) | falling slab, bricks from the ceiling, rolling boulder | |
| `darts` darts | 1 / 4 / 4 | 2 (2) | poisoned darts, poison needle in the lock, fusillade of darts | |

In all: 21 families, 65 ready-made traps (31 WyrmDelve's own, 34 from the System Reference Document 3.5), 25 mechanisms, 18 triggers and 11 twists, for **2,648 different traps**.

## 3. The path, step by step

Traps come **after** the dungeon has been built, and use dice of their own (made from the seed with the label `traps`): the map, the story and the monsters of a seed don't change whether you choose traps or not.

1. **Distances.** For every room, the program counts how many steps it takes to get there from outside, through corridors, stairs, shafts, portals and entrances. A corridor counts half a step more than the nearer of its two rooms.
2. **Possible spots.** On each level: every room, and the corridors at least 5 squares long that don't already have something special (water, cave-in, steps, secret passage).
3. **Picking the spots.** For each level, the program draws some spots, preferring the important ones (chapter 4).
4. **Picking the trap.** For each spot, a trap that fits: the builder (chapter 5), the level's type, the spot (room or corridor) and the stage for that depth (chapter 7). Two times in five it is a ready-made trap, the other times one assembled from its parts (chapter 8).
5. **The lesson.** For each family used, the program makes sure the players meet the broken version first (chapter 6), and adds it if needed.
6. **The square.** Each trap gets an exact square on the map: in a room, a free square away from the doors, the room number and other symbols; in a corridor, the middle square.

Every room and every corridor holds at most one trap.

---

## 4. How many traps and where

- **How many**: about one every 5 rooms per level, and at least one per level (sub-levels included), plus the broken traps that teach.
- **Where**: you protect what's valuable. The program draws the spots with these weights:

| Spot | Weight |
|---|---|
| **important** room (throne hall, burial chamber, lab, apse, keep…) | 3 |
| corridor leading to an important room | 2 |
| any other room or corridor | 1 |

A spot with weight 3 is three times as likely to be picked as one with weight 1.

---

## 5. Who built them

The builder depends on the spot's **age**, the same one the walls show:

| Spot's age | Walls | Builder | Traps |
|---|---|---|---|
| I founders | `═║` | **careful** (`built`) | mechanisms: pits, blocks, darts, blades, spears, nets, cages, levers, chutes, gas, fire, acid, runes, floods; triggers such as pressure plates, doors, locks and idols |
| II second age | `─│` | **careful** (`built`) | as above: those who came later hid their passages |
| N natural caves, III today | `#` | **crude** (`crude`) | bells, bear traps, snares, sacks of rocks, broken glass, pits covered with branches, spiked logs, jars of vermin, oil and spark; triggers such as tripwires, loose stones, bait and rotten planks |

So the traps tell the place's story: in the old halls there are mechanisms designed by architects, in the lairs of today's dwellers there are traps made from whatever was at hand.

## 6. Learn first, then risk

This is the heart of the algorithm. For every family of working traps (stages 1 and 2):

1. the program finds the working trap **closest to the outside**;
2. if there is already a stage-0 trap of the same family **even closer**, all is well;
3. otherwise it adds one in a free spot **before**, that is with fewer steps from outside, choosing the spot just before, to show the mechanism shortly before it matters;
4. if there is no room before, it tries at the same distance;
5. if there is no room there either (for instance, the trap is in the entrance room), that trap itself becomes the broken version.

The broken version comes from the ready-made stage-0 traps or from the broken version (`broken`) of a mechanism of the same family. Families with no broken version at all (for instance the crude ones, swarms, portcullises) have no lesson: they are simple, and their tells (stretched cords, humming jars, grooves in the walls) speak for themselves.

Distances count steps across the **whole** dungeon, not the level: if the main entrance is on level 2, the lesson can be on level 2 and the working trap on level 1, which is reached by climbing the stairs.

---

## 7. The deeper, the crueller

Crude traps are always stage 1. For the others, the chance of stage 2 grows with the level's depth:

| Level | Chance of stage 2 |
|---|---|
| 1 | 0% |
| 2 | 30% |
| sub-level 2a | 45% |
| 3 | 60% |
| 4 and beyond | 80% |

An assembled stage-2 trap is a mechanism with a cruel **twist** (chapter 8). If there is no fitting stage-2 trap for that spot, stage 1 is used.

## 8. Ready-made traps and assembled traps

The ready-made traps are few and written by hand. To have thousands, the program **assembles** new ones from three parts:

| Part | What it says | How many | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **trigger** (`triggers`) | what sets it off, and how you notice it | 18 | pressure plate, tripwire, door, chest lid, idol on the pedestal, false step, light, noise, lock, bait, rotten plank |
| **mechanism** (`mechanisms`) | what happens, with its tell and its broken version | 25 | darts from the walls, trapdoor, dropping cage, poison gas, acid spray, frost glyph, alarm gong, spiked log |
| **twist** (`twists`) | what makes it cruel (stage 2 only) | 11 | with poison, that resets, with a twin further on, that seals the room, with a decoy, with a hidden tell, with a monster at the bottom |

The parts combine only when they go together: the trigger and the mechanism must suit the same spot (room or corridor) and the same builder, the mechanism must suit the level's type (runes only in magical places, water only where there is some), and the twist must suit that mechanism (poison only on darts, blades, spears, pits and wires; a monster at the bottom only on pits and chutes). Some triggers rule out a mechanism: a chest lid doesn't open a chute.

The parts of the trap in the key are put together like this:

| Part | How it is made | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **name** | mechanism + twist + (trigger) | Spears from the wall with a needle for whoever disarms it (tripwire) |
| **tell** | the mechanism's tell; the trigger's; the twist's | fist-sized holes in a row, with chipped edges; a thin cord at ankle height that glints in the light |
| **effect** | the mechanism's effect, the twist's effect | spears shoot out of the holes and run through whoever passes, and whoever tries to disarm it by hand pricks a poisoned needle |
| **countermeasures** | the trigger's; the mechanism's; the twist's | step over the cord; cut it while holding it taut with one hand; duck under the row; wedge the spears with chocks; disarm it with long tools, never with the fingers |

The **lesson** (stage 0) of an assembled family is the mechanism's broken version: *broken spears*, *rusted crossbow*, *rusted cage*. Its effect and countermeasures are the same for all: the mechanism is broken, but it shows what it looks like.

How many different traps there are: 65 ready-made, 10 broken versions of the mechanisms and 2,573 combinations of trigger, mechanism and twist (or no twist) that go together. **2,648** in all.

---

## 9. On the map and in the key

- **Game master's map**: a bold `^` symbol on the trap's square, and an entry `^ trap (GM only)` in the legend.
- **Players' map**: nothing, not even in the legend.
- **Key** (`.txt` and PDF): traps in rooms are written under the room; traps in corridors are listed under the level's heading, in order of distance from the entrance, so the lesson comes first.
- **While generating**, the program says how many traps it placed: `7 traps (^ on the game master's map only)`.

| 1 | room 2-01 | I | broken spears | 0, the lesson |
| 1.5 | corridor 2-01 – 2-03 | I | spears from the wall with a needle for whoever disarms it (tripwire) | 2, assembled |
| 3 | room 1-03 | I | rusted crossbow | 0, the lesson |

## 11. Adding traps

Everything is in `data/traps.json`, one item per line. Make a copy of the file before you edit it.

### A ready-made trap (`traps`)

```json
{"family": "pit", "stage": 1, "places": ["room", "corridor"], "builders": "built", "where": ["*"], "name": {"it": "fossa nascosta", "en": "hidden pit"}, "tell": {"it": "le lastre del pavimento hanno fessure dritte e suonano vuote", "en": "the floor slabs have straight seams and sound hollow"}, "effect": {"it": "il pavimento si apre: una caduta profonda su un fondo di pietra", "en": "the floor opens: a deep fall onto a stone bottom"}, "counter": {"it": "sondare il pavimento con un'asta; saltarla; bloccare il coperchio con dei chiodi", "en": "probe the floor with a pole; jump it; nail the lid shut"}, "source": "wyrmdelve"},
```

| Field | What it holds | Values |
|---|---|---|
| `family` | the family | any name: traps with the same name teach each other |
| `stage` | the stage | `0` broken (it teaches), `1` working, `2` cruel |
| `places` | where it can be | `["room"]`, `["corridor"]` or both |
| `builders` | who builds it | `"built"` (founders and second age) or `"crude"` (today's dwellers) |
| `where` | in which dungeon types | `["*"]` everywhere, or for instance `["tomb", "temple"]` |
| `needs` | (optional) a condition | `"below"`: only if there is a level below |
| `name`, `tell`, `effect`, `counter` | name, tell, effect, countermeasures | in Italian (`it`) and in English (`en`) |
| `source` | (optional) where it comes from | `"wyrmdelve"`, `"srd35"` |

### A trigger (`triggers`)

```json
{"builders": ["built"], "places": ["room", "corridor"], "name": {"it": "lastra a pressione", "en": "pressure plate"}, "tell": {"it": "una lastra appena più alta delle altre, con il bordo pulito", "en": "a slab a little higher than the rest, with a clean edge"}, "counter": {"it": "non calpestare la lastra; bloccarla con un cuneo", "en": "don't step on the slab; jam it with a chock"}},
```

`builders` and `places` are lists; `avoid` (optional) lists the families the trigger makes no sense with, for instance `["slide"]`. In the countermeasures **name the object** ("don't step on the slab"), don't use a pronoun: the mechanism's countermeasures come after, and "don't step on it" would no longer be clear.

### A mechanism (`mechanisms`)

```json
{"family": "cage", "builders": ["built"], "places": ["room"], "where": ["*"], "name": {"it": "gabbia che scende", "en": "dropping cage"}, "tell": {"it": "quattro scanalature negli angoli…", "en": "four grooves in the corners…"}, "effect": {"it": "una gabbia di ferro cade…", "en": "an iron cage drops…"}, "counter": {"it": "restare vicino ai muri…", "en": "keep near the walls…"}, "broken": {"name": {"it": "gabbia arrugginita", "en": "rusted cage"}, "tell": {"it": "una gabbia caduta di traverso, bloccata a metà dalla ruggine", "en": "a cage fallen askew, stuck halfway by rust"}}},
```

The fields are those of the ready-made traps (without `stage`: the program decides it), with `builders` as a list. `broken` (optional) is its broken version, the lesson: name and tell. Without `broken`, the family can only get a lesson from a ready-made stage-0 trap.

### A twist (`twists`)

```json
{"families": ["darts", "blade", "spear", "pit", "wire"], "places": ["room", "corridor"], "name": {"it": "con veleno", "en": "with poison"}, "effect": {"it": "e le ferite sono avvelenate (tiro salvezza contro veleno)", "en": "and the wounds are poisoned (save versus poison)"}, "counter": {"it": "non toccare le punte a mani nude", "en": "don't touch the points bare-handed"}, "tell": {"it": "un odore amaro, di mandorle", "en": "a bitter smell, of almonds"}},
```

`families` are the families it can be added to (`["*"]` = all). `effect` and `tell` are optional; the effect comes after the mechanism's, so it starts with "and…", "then…" or "but…". The **name** comes after the mechanism's, which in Italian may be masculine or feminine, singular or plural: write it so it fits them all ("with poison", "that resets").

### Tips

- **A new family** works best with all three stages, or with a mechanism that has its broken version. Without stage 0, the "learn first" rule can't be kept for that family.
- **The tell** must describe the **mechanism** (what you see), not the danger (what happens).
- **Countermeasures** must be things the players can do with everyday gear: poles, ropes, spikes, wax, rags, stones to throw.
- **Stage 0** must make stage 1 understandable: the same mechanism, but exposed, broken or already sprung.
- For the rules on commas and quotes in the JSON file, see chapter 9 of [add_monsters_to_bestiary.en.md](add_monsters_to_bestiary.en.md): they apply here too.
