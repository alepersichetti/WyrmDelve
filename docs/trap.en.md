# How the traps are made

*[Versione italiana](trap.md)*

This document explains how WyrmDelve chooses, places and describes the traps of a dungeon, with real examples generated by the program.

> Traps only appear if at the start, at "What do you want to make?", you choose an answer with **traps** (or `--content traps`, also together with other things: `--content story,traps`). How the map they are placed on is made is explained in [dungeon_map_generation.en.md](dungeon_map_generation.en.md).

## 1. The idea: a trap is a problem, not a dice roll

The logic follows the principles of the post [*Some Traps*](https://goblinpunch.blogspot.com/2018/08/some-traps.html) by Goblin Punch (Arnold K.):

- **learn first**: before a hidden trap, the players must have seen the same mechanism exposed or broken;
- **telegraph the mechanism, not the danger**: there is always something to notice (seams, holes, a lever oiled too well), even if you don't know what will happen;
- **solve it by thinking**: with what they see, the players can avoid it, disarm it or turn it to their advantage, without relying on a dice roll.

That's why every trap, in the key, has four parts:

| Part | What it says | Example (hidden pit) |
|---|---|---|
| **name** | what it is | hidden pit |
| **tell** | what you notice, if you look | the floor slabs have straight seams and sound hollow |
| **effect** | what happens if it goes off | the floor opens: a deep fall onto a stone bottom |
| **countermeasures** | how to beat it by thinking | probe the floor with a pole; jump it; nail the lid shut |

The traps in the `wyrmdelve_tables.json` file are written for WyrmDelve: the post's principles are used, not its text.

## 2. Families and the three stages

A **family** is a mechanism. Each family has up to three **stages**:

| Stage | What it is |
|---|---|
| **0 — broken or exposed** | it does no harm: it shows how the mechanism works |
| **1 — working** | the hidden mechanism |
| **2 — cruel** | a variant that punishes whoever thinks they've figured it out |

The families in the file:

| Family | Stage 0 | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `pit` pits | exposed pit | hidden pit | double pit | rooms and corridors | |
| `block` blocks | fallen block | falling slab | descending ceiling | rooms and corridors (the ceiling only in rooms) | |
| `darts` darts | rusty dart holes | poisoned darts | dart gallery | rooms and corridors | |
| `blade` blades | stuck blade | swinging blade | scythe corridor | rooms and corridors | |
| `lever` levers | broken lever | lever under the trapdoor | three levers | rooms | |
| `slide` chutes | open chute | tilting floor | corridor chute | rooms and corridors | only if there is a level below to fall into |
| `gas` gas | broken nozzle | sleeping gas | sealed room | rooms | |
| `rune` runes | spent rune | fire rune | ring of glyphs | rooms and corridors | only in arcane laboratory, academy, temple, tower, tomb |
| `flood` floods | half-flooded room | flooding room | — | rooms | only in Underdark, tomb, temple, city, prison, castle |
| `crude` crude | — | alarm bells, bear trap, sack over the door, snare, floor of shards | — | rooms and corridors | today's dwellers' traps |

In all: 10 families, 31 traps.

## 3. The path, step by step

Traps come **after** the dungeon has been built, and use dice of their own (made from the seed with the label `traps`): the map, the story and the monsters of a seed don't change whether you choose traps or not.

1. **Distances.** For every room, the program counts how many steps it takes to get there from outside, through corridors, stairs, shafts, portals and entrances. A corridor counts half a step more than the nearer of its two rooms.
2. **Possible spots.** On each level: every room, and the corridors at least 5 squares long that don't already have something special (water, cave-in, steps, secret passage).
3. **Picking the spots.** For each level, the program draws some spots, preferring the important ones (chapter 4).
4. **Picking the trap.** For each spot, a trap that fits: the builder (chapter 5), the level's type, the spot (room or corridor) and the stage for that depth (chapter 7).
5. **The lesson.** For each family used, the program makes sure the players meet the broken version first (chapter 6), and adds it if needed.
6. **The square.** Each trap gets an exact square on the map: in a room, a free square away from the doors, the room number and other symbols; in a corridor, the middle square.

Every room and every corridor holds at most one trap.

---

## 4. How many traps and where

- **How many**: about one every 5 rooms per level, and at least one per level (sub-levels included), plus the broken traps that teach.
- **Where**: you protect what's valuable. The program draws the spots with these weights:

| Spot | Weight |
|---|---|
| **important** room (throne hall, burial chamber, lab, apse, keep…) | 3 |
| corridor leading to an important room | 2 |
| any other room or corridor | 1 |

A spot with weight 3 is three times as likely to be picked as one with weight 1.

---

## 5. Who built them

The builder depends on the spot's **age**, the same one the walls show:

| Spot's age | Walls | Builder | Traps |
|---|---|---|---|
| I founders | `═║` | **careful** (`built`) | mechanisms: pits, blocks, darts, blades, levers, chutes, gas, runes, floods |
| II second age | `─│` | **careful** (`built`) | as above: those who came later hid their passages |
| N natural caves, III today | `#` | **crude** (`crude`) | bells, bear traps, snares, sacks of rocks, broken glass |

So the traps tell the place's story: in the old halls there are mechanisms designed by architects, in the lairs of today's dwellers there are traps made from whatever was at hand.

---

## 6. Learn first, then risk

This is the heart of the algorithm. For every family of working traps (stages 1 and 2):

1. the program finds the working trap **closest to the outside**;
2. if there is already a stage-0 trap of the same family **even closer**, all is well;
3. otherwise it adds one in a free spot **before**, that is with fewer steps from outside, choosing the spot just before, to show the mechanism shortly before it matters;
4. if there is no room before, it tries at the same distance;
5. if there is no room there either (for instance, the trap is in the entrance room), that trap itself becomes the broken version.

Crude traps have no stage 0: they are simple, and their tells (stretched threads, heaps of rags) speak for themselves.

Distances count steps across the **whole** dungeon, not the level: if the main entrance is on level 2, the lesson can be on level 2 and the working trap on level 1, which is reached by climbing the stairs.

---

## 7. The deeper, the crueller

Crude traps are always stage 1. For the others, the chance of stage 2 grows with the level's depth:

| Level | Chance of stage 2 |
|---|---|
| 1 | 0% |
| 2 | 30% |
| sub-level 2a | 45% |
| 3 | 60% |
| 4 and beyond | 80% |

If there is no fitting stage-2 trap for that spot (for instance, floods have no stage 2), stage 1 is used.

---

## 8. On the map and in the key

- **Game master's map**: a bold `^` symbol on the trap's square, and an entry `^ trap (GM only)` in the legend.
- **Players' map**: nothing, not even in the legend.
- **Key** (`.txt` and PDF): traps in rooms are written under the room; traps in corridors are listed under the level's heading, in order of distance from the entrance, so the lesson comes first.
- **While generating**, the program says how many traps it placed: `7 traps (^ on the game master's map only)`.

| 1.5 | corridor 2-01 – 2-03 | I | exposed pit | 0, the lesson |
| 2 | room 3-02 | I | hidden pit | 1 |
| 2.5 | corridor 2-03 – 2-05 | I | hidden pit | 1 |
| 2.5 | corridor 3-07 – 3-08 | N | floor of shards | crude |

```

    in plain sight, full of bones. Effect: none, if it is looked at: it shows there are
    hidden pits here. Countermeasures: walk round it along the edge.
  ^ between 2-03 and 2-05: Hidden pit. Tell: the floor slabs have straight seams and sound
    hollow. Effect: the floor opens: a deep fall onto a stone bottom. Countermeasures:
    probe the floor with a pole; jump it; nail the lid shut.
```

```
the floor opens: a deep fall onto a stone bottom. Countermeasures: probe the floor with a pole;
jump it; nail the lid shut.
```

## 10. Adding traps

Traps are in the `traps` table of `wyrmdelve_tables.json`, one per line. For example:

```json
{"family": "pit", "stage": 1, "places": ["room", "corridor"], "builders": "built", "where": ["*"], "name": {"it": "fossa nascosta", "en": "hidden pit"}, "tell": {"it": "le lastre del pavimento hanno fessure dritte e suonano vuote", "en": "the floor slabs have straight seams and sound hollow"}, "effect": {"it": "il pavimento si apre: una caduta profonda su un fondo di pietra", "en": "the floor opens: a deep fall onto a stone bottom"}, "counter": {"it": "sondare il pavimento con un'asta; saltarla; bloccare il coperchio con dei chiodi", "en": "probe the floor with a pole; jump it; nail the lid shut"}},
```

| Field | What it holds | Values |
|---|---|---|
| `family` | the family | any name: traps with the same name teach each other |
| `stage` | the stage | `0` broken (it teaches), `1` working, `2` cruel |
| `places` | where it can be | `["room"]`, `["corridor"]` or both |
| `builders` | who builds it | `"built"` (founders and second age) or `"crude"` (today's dwellers) |
| `where` | in which dungeon types | `["*"]` everywhere, or for example `["tomb", "temple"]` |
| `needs` | (optional) a condition | `"below"`: only if there is a level below |
| `name`, `tell`, `effect`, `counter` | name, tell, effect, countermeasures | in Italian (`it`) and in English (`en`) |

Tips:

- **A new family** works best with all three stages. Without stage 0, the "learn first" rule can't be kept for that family.
- **The tell** must describe the **mechanism** (what you see), not the danger (what happens).
- **Countermeasures** should be things players can do with everyday gear: poles, ropes, spikes, wax, rags, stones to throw.
- **Stage 0** must make stage 1 understandable: the same mechanism, but exposed, broken or already sprung.
- For the rules on commas and quotes in the JSON file, see chapter 9 of [add_monsters_to_bestiary.en.md](add_monsters_to_bestiary.en.md): they apply here too.
