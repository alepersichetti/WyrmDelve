# How the monsters are made

*[Versione italiana](monsters.md)*

This document explains how WyrmDelve populates a dungeon: the wandering monster tables, the monsters in the rooms, the clues in the empty rooms and the out-of-place monsters. The examples were generated by the program.

> Monsters only appear if at the start, at "What do you want to make?", you choose an answer with **monsters** (or `--content monsters`, also together with other things). To **add** monsters, clues and reasons to the JSON file there is a separate guide: [add_monsters_to_bestiary.en.md](add_monsters_to_bestiary.en.md).

## 1. In short

With monsters, the dungeon key (`.txt` and PDF) has:

- for every level, below its heading, a **d6 wandering monster table**;
- for every room, **what is inside**: monsters, a clue, or nothing.

Everything is decided **after** the dungeon has been built, with dice of its own (made from the seed with the label `monsters`): the map and the story of a seed don't change whether you choose monsters or not, and the same seed always gives the same monsters.

The monsters are in the `monsters` table of `wyrmdelve_tables.json` (245 monsters): each one says where it can appear (`where`), how dangerous it is (`danger`, 1 to 4), what kind of creature it is (`kind`) and how many you meet (`number`).

---

## 2. Danger grows with depth

Every level has a **danger** from 1 to 4: the first level has danger 1, and each level further down adds one point, up to 4. With more than 4 levels danger grows more slowly, so that it reaches 4 only on the last level.

| Levels in the dungeon | Danger level by level |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1 |
| 3 | 1, 2, 3 |
| 4 | 1, 2, 3, 4 |
| 6 | 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4 |

A sub-level takes the danger of its depth, halfway between its two levels, rounded.

To pick the monsters of a level the program looks, in this order, for:

1. monsters that fit the level's **type** with **the same danger**;
2. if there aren't enough, those one point above or below;
3. then any monster that fits the type;
4. and only at the end any monster at all.

## 3. The wandering monster table

Roll a d6 when the characters make noise or waste time.

- **1** is always **today's dwellers** (the story's stratum III), roaming the halls: the dungeon is their home.
- **2–6** are five monsters picked as in chapter 2: spiders and oozes in the caves, undead in the tombs, demons and golems in the arcane laboratory, watchmen and thugs in the city.
- **No repeats**: as long as there are new ones, a monster already used on one level doesn't come back on another.
- If the level has an **out-of-place monster** (chapter 5), it takes slot 6, with a reference to its room.

---

## 4. The rooms: monsters, clues, empty rooms

A good dungeon isn't full of monsters: empty rooms give breathing space, build tension and leave room for exploration. Level by level:

1. **About a third of the rooms** hold monsters (35%, rounded, at least one), and **at least a third always stay empty**.
2. **Today's dwellers** live in the rooms that have a third-age layer (`III:` in the description): those are their lairs, up to half of the occupied rooms.
3. If there is an **out-of-place monster**, it takes a built room (founders' or second age), if one is free.
4. **The other monsters** of the wandering table take the remaining rooms: the table says who roams, the rooms say where they sleep.
5. For **every room with monsters**, the nearest empty room (first those joined by a corridor, then those in the same part of the level, then the closest) gets a **clue** matching that creature's kind (`kind`): bones gnawed by human teeth for the undead, slimy trails for water creatures, a smell of sulphur for demons, signs of passage for today's dwellers. The clue says which room it comes from.
6. The other rooms are simply **empty**.

Next to every monster is **how many** you meet: usually 2d6 for weak monsters, then 1d6, 1d3 and finally 1 for the most dangerous; no number for swarms.

In the key:

| Text | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Monsters: Bear (1d6).` | a monster's lair |
| `Monsters: the bugbears of … (2d6): this is their lair.` | today's dwellers' lair |
| `Monsters: Aboleth (1). Out of place: …` | an out-of-place monster, with the reason |
| `Empty. Clue: fresh paw prints in the dust (from 2-02).` | an empty room with a clue |
| `Empty.` | an empty room |

## 5. Out-of-place monsters

A dungeon that is too tidy is predictable. That is why **about one level in three** (3 times out of 10) hosts a monster that **doesn't belong** to the level's type, and the key says why it is there. The reason comes from what the dungeon really has: a reason only applies if its condition (`when` in the `quirks` table) is true.

| `when` | Condition | Reason (example) | Monster |
|---|---|---|---|
| `water` | another level has natural caves and water (preferably deeper) | the flooded caves of level 3 reach up under this room, through a pool | a water creature |
| `portal` | the dungeon has a magic portal | the portal in 1-04 now and then lets something through | any |
| `below` | the level below is of another type | things often come up from level 3 to hunt | one that fits the level below |
| `founders` | the level has founders' rooms | the founders left a guardian here, and no one ever revoked the order | a construct |
| `any` | always | a spell gone wrong opened a rift; today's dwellers keep what they catch chained here; a recent fight; a hidden treasure; corridors where it's easy to get lost | depending on the reason |

The out-of-place monster can be somewhat more dangerous than the level (up to two points more): it's a surprise, and the game master decides how to use it. That's how, for instance, an **aboleth in a castle pool** can turn up, come up from the flooded caves of the level below.

Example (seed `4-42-4-2-F-003ADB`, a 4-level city, level 3):

```
  3-10 Monsters: Fish folk (2d6). Out of place: a cistern under the floor connects with the
  underground rivers of level 2.
```

## 6. A complete example

Level 2 of seed `3-21-2-4-CDK-000F62` (a castle), with story and monsters:

```
LEVEL 2 — CASTLE
  Wandering monsters (d6)
    1. The bugbears of Inchalville: today's dwellers, roaming the halls.
    2. Bear: a hungry bear that came in through the ruins.
    3. Panther: a black cat, sometimes kept as a guard animal.
    4. Mimic: looks like a chest or a door, until it bites.
    5. Doppelganger: takes the look of whoever stands before it and slips into the party.
    6. Werewolf: by day a person like any other, by night a wolf.
  2-01 [I] Prison tower; III: bugbear den.
         Monsters: the bugbears of Inchalville (2d6): this is their lair.
         exits: → 2-03 (opening); → 2-02 (secret passage); > stairs down → 3-02; entrance A
  2-02 [I] Corner tower; II: memories room.
         Monsters: Bear (1d6).
         exits: → 2-03 (door); → 2-01 (secret passage); → 2-05 (door, steps); > stairs down → 2a-01
  2-03 [I] Stable yard.
         Empty. Clue: a heap of bones and stolen goods, someone's loot (from 2-01).
         exits: → 2-04 (door); → 2-01 (door); → 2-05 (door); → 2-02 (door); < stairs up → 1-03
  2-04 [I] Banner tower.
         Empty.
         exits: → 2-03 (door); < stairs up → 1-05
  2-05 [I] Round tower; II: common kitchen.
         Empty. Clue: fresh paw prints in the dust (from 2-02).
         exits: → 2-03 (door); → 2-02 (door, steps); > stairs down → 3-03
```

- The table has today's dwellers at 1 and five castle monsters of danger 2 (or close to it).
- `2-01` is a third-age room (*bugbear den*): today's dwellers live there.
- `2-02` is the bear's lair, the first monster of the table.
- `2-03` and `2-05` are empty, but with a clue: each is joined by a corridor to one of the two lairs.
- `2-04` is just empty.

## 7. Where the monsters come from

The list was drawn up by comparing two bestiaries, *OSR Bestiary* (bucolian) and *Aketon*'s *Monstrous Bestiary* (Reese Surles), and removing the duplicates: for instance Aketon's *Ruster* is the rust monster, the *Gel Cube* is the gelatinous cube, the *Landshark* is the bulette. The two bestiaries carry no open license, so only the creatures' names and Hit Dice (used for the danger) come from them: **every description is written for WyrmDelve**. The aboleth comes from the SRD 5.1 (CC BY 4.0). Monsters whose names are trademarks of others, named demon lords and animals that make no sense in a dungeon (whales, horses, dinosaurs…) were left out. The full sources are in the README.
